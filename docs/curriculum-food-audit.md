# Food, home and travel curriculum audit — draft

Updated: 2026-11-15. All text is an original AI-assisted editorial draft requiring owner review. The level labels estimate the demands of the task; they do not certify CEFR coverage. No native-speaker, classroom, audible speech or owner publication review has taken place.

## Scope and practical design

Reviewed every example, translation, outcome, dialogue, grammar note and sentence exercise in the 28 existing short lessons in `food-home.ts`. The two existing Restaurant/Bakery starter lessons are owned by the primary integrator; this audit does not alter them. Added ten original short lessons in `food-travel.ts`. Each topic has A1 and A2 work, mostly present-tense requests before A2 comparisons, checking and problem resolution. Lessons contain six or seven targets, with directly visible English and optional grammar support. Existing answer keys and lesson IDs remain unchanged.

| Topic | Targets including starter | Coherent clusters and practical purpose | Prerequisites and deliberate recycling |

## What changed and why

The principal weakness was missing actions and conversation closure, not that ordinary food, furniture or transport nouns were off-topic. Shorter original examples now isolate an immediately usable utterance instead of requiring a second clause or incidental vocabulary merely to meet a word-count target. All 28 exchanges were rewritten or completed with a concrete reply; rental/repair/collection/connection exchanges now reach an agreed next step. Every existing outcome was sharpened to an observable task. Existing sentence tokens and answers were reviewed and retained, including the original sofa sentence; no existing accepted alternative was removed.

Useful nouns originally considered for replacement were retained as seventh items: Restaurant person/chicken/milk/knife; Groceries banana/sugar/cream/trolley; Shopping coat/hat/colour; Housing living room/wardrobe/stairs/tenant/repair; Transport tram/metro station/ticket counter/timetable/information. This preserves their correct stable IDs and existing illustrations while adding the missing functional items under fresh IDs.

Three old targets were removed from the active banks; no old ID is reassigned to a new sense:

| Removed stable ID and target | Replacement | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `fr.bakery.rye-bread` — du pain de seigle | `fr.bakery.which-bread` — quel pain | A third named bread style adds less ability to choose than a question used with any available loaf. Existing country/wholemeal bread and starter baguette remain. |
| `fr.bakery.chouquette` — une chouquette | `fr.bakery.anything-else` — et avec ça ? | Prioritises understanding the baker’s next question over a fifth sweet-product name. Existing pastry variety remains. The primary integrator retains its historical illustration binding for older notebook entries. |
| `fr.groceries.use-by-date` — une date limite de consommation | `fr.groceries.check` — vérifier | The previous lesson mixed a specialised label distinction with a checkout-price task. General checking is useful within that task. Food-date literacy remains a future bounded lesson needing authoritative food-label research, rather than an unsupported safety claim here. |

Added distinct task targets: Restaurant `book-table`, `ill-have`, `contain`, `bring`; Bakery `which-bread`, `anything-else`; Groceries `need`, `buy`, `how-much-cost`, `look-for`, `check`; Shopping `look-for-item`, `wear`, `another-colour`; Housing `live-in`, `put`, `open`, `move-in`, `arrange-visit`; Transport `take-bus`, `get-on`, `buy-ticket`, `change-trains`, `how-long`. The sense slug is new whenever the meaning changes. Shared contracts, alias routing, starter content and catalogue exports remain the primary agent’s responsibility.

## Sense reuse and source limits

Explicit exact-target same-sense pair for central alias integration: `fr.shopping.look-for-item` → `fr.groceries.look-for`, both `chercher` in the sense “look for an item”. The shopping row records the recycling. Integration scan also found `fr.restaurant.bring` / `fr.social.bring` (apporter), `fr.groceries.check` / `fr.work.verifier` (vérifier), `fr.housing.live-in` / `fr.people.habiter` (habiter), `fr.housing.open` / `fr.education.ouvrir` (physical opening), `fr.accommodation.password` / `fr.administration.mot-de-passe` / `fr.digital.password` (password), and `fr.bakery.tomorrow` / `fr.calendar.tomorrow` (demain). The primary integrator controls their canonical aliases and the four existing shared aliases.

Other new chunks extend a known verb with a useful complement (book a table/room, buy/buy a ticket, put/put in the refrigerator); those targets differ and should not be forced into an exact-target alias. Restaurant napkin `une serviette` and Accommodation bath towel `une serviette de bain` are separate contextual senses. Other intentional distinctions are clothing fit versus social agreement (`ça me va`), temporal until versus spatial as far as (`jusqu’à`), physical versus digital opening (`ouvrir`), transport versus administration counters (`un guichet`), and transport versus work schedules (`un horaire`). Existing `fr.restaurant.receipt` and `fr.shopping.proof-of-purchase` are closely related receipt expressions with different forms; no silent alias or target rewrite was made.

Actually inspected 2026-11-15:

- [Council of Europe CEFR global scale](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale): performance orientation for simple concrete needs at A1 and routine exchanges at A2. This is evidence for task demand, not an official inventory of French words or the certification of these lessons.
- [TV5MONDE vocabulary index](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr/aides/vocabulaire): confirms thematic A1/A2 organisation and visible A1 meal, kitchen and clothing topic entries. Used as independent scope inspiration, not as a copied vocabulary collection or a validation of each selected word.
- [CLE International beginner vocabulary resource page](https://progressive.cle-international.com/9782090398489): publisher identifies adult/adolescent beginner A1 material and exposes communicative activity labels including holidays and a recipe. Used as a second independent FLE reference for practical topic/task orientation. No course audio, chapter inventories, exercises, examples or illustrations were downloaded or reused.

Accommodation’s detailed service/check-out sequence is an editorial inference from practical newcomer needs, supported only broadly by the inspected holiday/task orientation; the three sources do not validate every hotel expression. The same limit applies to individual lexical choices in all topics. External dictionary/corpus checks were not performed. All examples, dialogues, explanations and activities were composed for this repository. No new media is included. Retained code-drawn media keeps its existing rights record; a generic notebook remains meaning matching, never a claim to depict an abstract verb.

## Validation and owner-review questions

Content checks compare existing lesson/sense identities, preserve old sentence keys, verify token solvability, count distinct targets and scan repaired UTF-8 French. Combined repository checks, export/browser behaviour and canonicalisation are the primary integrator’s responsibility and are not asserted here.

Owner review should check: whether A1 `j’en prends deux` and `se laver les mains` need extra foundation support; whether to teach hotel `faut-il libérer` as a fixed A2 formula; scope of short allergy requests without implying a safety guarantee; the distinction between cooking intransitive `cuire` and causative `faire cuire`; the appropriate number of room/utensil names for this learner. Naturalness, accent accuracy, register, estimated levels, sentence alternatives and unchanged illustrations still need owner/teacher preview. No unresolved specific gender disagreement was found by editorial review; that is not native-speaker validation.
