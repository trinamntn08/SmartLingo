# French lesson pacing and practice review

Reviewed on 2026-11-24 for English-speaking adult beginners living in France. The review covered all 133 bundled French topic lessons in 27 banks plus the five French guided lessons. The topic inventory contains 7 foundation, 71 A1 and 55 A2 drafts. The [course catalogue](../COURSE_CATALOGUE.md) lists every title, goal and target set. The [research pack](README.md) documents the earlier 123-lesson progression and its lexical decisions.

## A realistic study session

| Phase | Topic lesson | Guided lesson | What the learner does |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Read the dialogue and language note | 2 min | 2 min | Read for the situation and the speaker’s intention. Read one role aloud if useful. |
| Study the key phrases | 3 min | 3 min | Work through the examples, identify a reusable phrase and recall it once. |
| Listen, match and build a sentence | 3–4 min | 3–4 min | Replay a line as needed. Use the transcript for support, then build a useful phrase. |
| Apply the goal to a new real-life situation | 2–3 min | — | In a topic lesson, respond before revealing the model, compare the reply and try again. |
| Total estimate | 10–12 min | About 10 min | Learners can take longer, repeat a step or move on. |

These are first-pass planning estimates for working through the activities with a short retry. Actual time will depend on reading fluency, audio access, help used and familiarity with French. The estimates are SmartLingo editorial decisions. At foundation and A1, one clear request, reply or exchange is enough. A2 practice can add a constraint, a reason, a second detail or a predictable follow-up. A simple report of a problem fits the current route. Negotiating a solution or making a sustained complaint belongs in a later course.

The topic reader offers an original production task that changes a detail or context. Models and short success criteria are available after the learner tries. Speaking, silent rehearsal and writing all work. In the guided opening, the sentence exercise and brief feedback finish each lesson. The bilingual dialogue and key phrases supply the study model.

## Bank-by-bank teaching focus

The task set was checked against each lesson’s outcome, vocabulary, dialogue and existing sentence exercise. These are the production goals across the resulting lesson sets.

| Topic bank | Lessons | Practice focus and progression |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Everyday greetings and politeness | 3 | Match a greeting or farewell to time of day. Address a stranger and a familiar person appropriately. |
| Essential conversation tools | 5 | Recover a missed detail, get help, express a limitation, ask a focused question and check understanding. Recycle these moves in later topics. |
| Numbers and quantities | 5 | Produce quantities, strings of digits, prices and totals in short exchanges. Learners still need compound-price and varied number listening. |
| People and introductions | 5 | Give a name, exchange identities, describe origins or a connection, and ask a simple settling-in question. |
| Dates and availability | 5 | Move from weekdays and months to confirming a complete date and giving availability. Month sets work best when revisited in an appointment. |
| Time and daily routine | 5 | Give a time, describe when an action happens and connect two actions in sequence. Use clock-time practice before longer routine summaries. |
| Finding your way on foot | 5 | Locate a service or landmark and give or clarify a short route. A map-based comprehension check would measure a separate skill. |
| Transport | 5 | Choose a mode, confirm a stop, request a ticket, check a connection and ask about an alternative after a disruption. |
| Bakery | 5 | Order politely, refine a bread or pastry order, request takeaway and confirm a collection. |
| Groceries | 5 | Specify a weight, package or portion, find a product and ask the cashier to check a price discrepancy. |
| Restaurant | 5 | Order and ask for the bill, request a table, choose a meal, state a fictional allergy and report a simple problem. Use a confirmation question for the allergy scenario. |
| Clothes and shopping | 5 | Request a garment, size or colour and explain a predictable exchange or return. |
| Home and housing | 5 | Describe rooms or furniture, ask a rental question and report a fault with a practical request. Use fictional housing situations. |
| Leisure and weather | 5 | Describe a familiar activity or weather and explain a change of plan. Keep weather and activity phrases connected by a decision. |
| Invitations and shared plans | 5 | Invite, respond, confirm details or suggest a workable alternative. Reuse dates and times. |
| Health and appointments | 5 | Request or change an appointment, report a fictional symptom and practise clarification with a professional. Keep the lesson focused on communication. |
| Cooking | 5 | Name ingredients, utensils and actions, give a short instruction and sequence preparation steps. |
| Phone and digital communication | 5 | Handle a missed call, give or check a detail and pass on a short message. Use fictional contact details. |
| Accommodation | 5 | Ask about a room, manage check-in or a service request, and report a predictable issue. |
| Education and learning | 5 | Ask about enrolment, understand a class need, ask for help or describe a study goal. |
| Work and study | 5 | Describe work or a schedule and clarify a simple task, deadline or instruction. |
| Services and paperwork | 5 | Explain why you came, check a fictional detail and ask how to supply a missing item. Use fictional paperwork. |
| Finding a job | 5 | Describe a goal, experience, availability or skill in short supported exchanges. Save a complete interview for later. |
| Talking about recent experiences | 5 | Add time, sequence and concrete detail to a short account. Prefer reusable past-tense chunks before longer connected speech. |
| Preferences and simple comparisons | 5 | State a preference, compare relevant features and support a joint choice with a short reason. |
| Getting around by bike | 5 | Ask about a rental, give a route or parking detail, and explain a predictable bike problem. |
| Sorting and disposing of household waste | 5 | Identify a container and ask how or where to dispose of something. Keep collection rules specific to the fictional setting or ask the local service. |

The five guided lessons build one chain of interactions. Learners greet a neighbour, exchange names, spell and correct a name, recover a missed detail, then confirm a workshop time. A future cumulative unit review could combine name, room and time at one reception, following the [A1 course map](french-daily-a1.md). The [earlier bridge drafts](bridge-notes.md) remain candidates for gaps such as compound prices. A longer guided route, varied unheard audio, cross-lesson retrieval, pronunciation and learner timing still need separate review.

## Teaching references

Inspected on 2026-11-24. The A1 design follows the Council of Europe’s focus on clear, supported daily exchanges. A2 practices predictable transactions and adds a condition, option or short reason where the goal calls for it. These descriptors inform task demands and level estimates. [Council of Europe descriptor search](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors-search) · [Obtaining goods and services scale](https://rm.coe.int/common-europeanframework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

Two adult-learning references informed the progression from model to practice. TV5MONDE groups greetings and leave-taking inside its A1 pathway. Didier’s A2 overview uses everyday materials, guided language focus, active tasks and unit review. SmartLingo’s tasks and examples are original. [TV5MONDE Première classe greeting exercises](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr/exercices/a1-debutant/vocabulaire-saluer-et-prendre-conge) · [Didier FLE Édito A2 teaching overview](https://didierfle.com/produit/edito-a2-edition-2022-2024-livre-didierfle-app/)

For a timing trial, observe first-time learners on a phone and a desktop across foundation, A1 transactions and A2 problem-solving. Record active time by phase, whether learners used a transcript or model, any unclear prompt and whether they could express the intended reply. Review the draft French models and English glosses before publication. Self-review and completion record different things. See the [content guide](../CONTENT_GUIDE.md).
