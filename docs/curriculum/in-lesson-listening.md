# In-lesson listening practice

Original SmartLingo activity design for adult learners. Draft, pending curriculum review.

## What the learner practises

The learner listens for something they could use in the situation, such as a greeting, a name, a request, a time or a next action. Foundation and A1 checks favour one clear utterance and one recognisable purpose or detail. At A2, a task can ask learners to pick out a practical condition or intention in a routine situation. The current Japanese levels retain their existing editorial estimates. A single practice attempt does not establish proficiency.

Study presents the bilingual dialogue and key phrases directly. Listening starts with an English question and short answer choices. Learners can replay the target sentence or open its transcript. Feedback identifies the answer and shows the target sentence and its English meaning, with a kana reading where available. The read-through route gives learners a way to continue when audio is unavailable. Kana rows instead ask learners to match a sound to a written character and offer the romaji reading on demand.

## Coverage and authoring

Write a question that the sound can answer on its own. Prefer a practical detail over a general translation where the lesson supports it. Keep alternative answers plausible for the same situation, distinct in meaning and free of clues unrelated to the audio. Reuse the existing lesson's words, pronunciation metadata and English phrasing. Where a question checks the whole sentence, choose alternatives from the same lesson and remove ambiguous mappings. A revised dialogue needs review before reusing a prompt that targets a specific detail.

All 133 French and 69 Japanese short lessons have an item. Five short lessons use individually drafted intent or detail questions. The other 197 ask learners to recognise a line's English meaning using contrasts from that lesson. All five French and thirteen Japanese guided lessons have individually drafted checks. For example, guided French learners listen for the spelling of a name or a workshop time. Later guided Japanese learners identify the application deadline or the condition for booking a room. The twenty kana rows share sound recognition across their authored characters.

The short-lesson reader, guided courses and kana recognition all use the app's existing playback. Browser speech and already available clips depend on the learner's device and media settings. Transcripts and ordinary lesson progression remain usable offline. Listening checks are practice. Existing learner and course completion rules continue to measure the existing recorded tasks.

## Sources consulted on 2026-11-23

- The Council of Europe's oral comprehension descriptors support short, clear input about immediately useful situations at A1 and comprehension of routine messages and concrete needs at A2. SmartLingo uses them to choose task demands. [CEFR Companion Volume, pp. 48–52](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4#page=48)
- TV5MONDE's early greetings series uses replay and recognition of expressions actually heard. It informed the foundation level's focus on greeting intent. [TV5MONDE Première classe](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr/exercices/a1-debutant/vocabulaire-saluer-et-prendre-conge)
- France Éducation international's A1 adult candidate guide illustrates brief messages, instructions and mini-dialogues, with a second pass to check key information. It informed the choice of practical details. [DELF A1 candidate guide, pp. 11–12](https://france-education-international.fr/document/manuel-candidat-delf-a1#page=11)
- Irodori introduces the situation and listening goal before asking for necessary information, then supports replay and script review. It informed the Japanese listening flow. [The Japan Foundation, How to Use Irodori Starter, pp. 8–9](https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/assets/data/starter/pdf/X_howto_en.pdf#page=8)

These sources informed the design. SmartLingo uses its own dialogue, examples and questions and does not reproduce the source exercises or audio.
