# French daily course and library practice

Reviewed 2026-11-22 for the five draft days of **First steps in France**, for English-speaking adults new to French. The guided sequence builds from greeting and leave-taking to exchanging names, spelling a name, asking for a slower repetition, and checking a day and time. Each day gives the learner one practical outcome and recycles earlier phrases where the situation calls for them. The linked library lessons offer optional repetition or transfer to a nearby situation. Keep their completion records separate from guided day completion.

## Reviewed links

The table lists the linked lesson IDs in display order. Each link was reviewed against the guided outcome and the library lesson's vocabulary, dialogue and activities. See the [runtime mapping](../../packages/content/src/daily-course-practice.ts).

| Guided day | Optional library lessons | What carries over |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Meet a neighbour | `fr.greetings.hello-goodbye`, `fr.greetings.check-in` | A neighbour exchange, arrival and departure formulas, a polite check-in and returning the question with *et vous ?* |
| 2. Introduce yourself | `fr.people.first-meeting` | Polite greetings, *je m’appelle*, name vocabulary and a closing wish in a different first meeting. The library sentence activity practises giving a name. The guided day also practises asking for someone else's name. |
| 3. Spell your name | `fr.essentials.clarify`, `fr.administration.contact-details` | The first dialogue reverses the role so the learner requests and checks a spelling. The second has the learner give letters at reception in a different context, spelling a street name. |
| 4. Ask someone to repeat | `fr.essentials.repair` | The request *Vous pouvez répéter plus lentement ?* and *j’ai compris* carry over from a name to a short code. The guided day adds the read-back check. |
| 5. Confirm a time | `fr.health.appointment`, `fr.calendar.working-week` | The appointment dialogue reuses *À quelle heure ?*, *à dix heures* and *demain*. The weekday dialogue models saying back a weekday with a clock time. |

The linked activities offer scaffolded practice. Spelling practice uses a few model names and a street name. Wider letter-name recognition and unsupported oral performance remain separate review questions.

## Sources inspected on 2026-11-22

- The Council of Europe places simple exchanges about personal details and basic everyday needs within supported A1 interaction. Its A1 self-assessment grid describes interaction with a partner who can repeat or rephrase slowly. These are reference points for the draft tasks. [CEFR global scale](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale) · [CEFR Companion volume, Appendix 2](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4)
- TV5MONDE uses greeting and leave-taking in an A1 track and places telling the time within a beginner appointment sequence. This supports reviewing time expressions in a second everyday situation. [Greetings and leave-taking](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr/exercices/a1-debutant/vocabulaire-saluer-et-prendre-conge) · [Telling the time](https://apprendre.tv5monde.com/fr/exercices/a1-debutant/grammaire-dire-lheure)
- Hachette FLE places giving and spelling names near the opening of its adult A1 course. This is an independent example of the link between introductions and spelling. [Cosmopolite 1 contents, p. 1](https://hachettefle.pl/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Cosmopolite_1_spis_tresci.pdf)

The links are SmartLingo editorial choices. All activities are existing SmartLingo drafts and keep their established provenance. This review assigns no proficiency or publication status.
