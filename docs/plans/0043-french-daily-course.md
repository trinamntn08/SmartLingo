# SmartLingo French daily course

Planned 2026-11-22 for English-speaking adults who are new to France. The product is a guided course that learners can study directly on SmartLingo. Learners get one recommended lesson per study day and can revisit or explore other lessons at any time. The route begins with the two existing pilot lessons. Every added lesson is written independently around a practical task, with an English interface and visible English study support.

## Courses, units and daily lessons

| Course | Units | Study days | Intended result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [A1 · First conversations and everyday essentials](../curriculum/french-daily-a1.md) | 8 | 56, D001–D056 | Handle short, predictable exchanges with a cooperative speaker and recognize key details in simple messages. |
| [A2 · Everyday independence](../curriculum/french-daily-a2.md) | 8 | 56, D057–D112 | Handle familiar transactions and changes, give a short explanation and send practical messages. |
| [B1 · Explain, solve and participate](../curriculum/french-daily-b1.md) | 8 | 56, D113–D168 | Follow clear input about familiar situations, recount events, compare options, and give reasons in short connected French. |

The 168-day, 24-unit schedule is a planning baseline. Level labels refer to the estimated demands of the tasks. Finishing the route records study activity. CEFR proficiency requires separate assessment. The A1 course includes the foundation opening in its 56 days. The D-numbers identify planning rows across the whole route. At runtime, each course can number its 56 days from 1–56 and retain stable course, day and activity IDs independent of display order.

An *unité* is a seven-day sequence with one communicative goal. Days 1–5 develop the goal in steps. Day 6 mixes earlier skills with the current situation, including the simple location question that completes the opening week's preparation. Day 7 applies the week's skills to a changed scene and includes review. Each day is one *leçon*. Short practice sections belong to that day's lesson. Transfers can carry a few expressions forward from earlier units. For example, A1 introduces confirming a time, A2 uses it to reschedule, and B1 uses it to negotiate a practical solution and explain why it works. Reading, listening, speaking and writing recur across the route. B1 also uses tasks that ask learners to relay the important information to another person.

## What one lesson contains

| Section | Authoring requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Scenario and outcome | One realistic scene, role and observable outcome. Optional generated image or a short accessible context description. |
| Study | A short original dialogue, message or notice in French with visible English. Select 6–10 useful items, including recycled items, with meanings, examples, gender/articles where relevant and brief usage notes. Map existing canonical senses before adding new IDs. |
| Grammar and sound | One small explanation tied to the task, such as a polite question frame or an audible distinction. Use familiar examples to explain it. Provide replay where supported and text support for every script. |
| Guided practice | One or more short choices, meaning matches or sentence-building tasks as supported by the course screen. Include context-specific feedback and reviewed answer alternatives. Keep deliberate reveals and hints in practice. |
| Use it | One changed situation in which the learner speaks, writes, selects and explains, or relays a message. Provide a labelled model and a concrete self-review checklist. Preserve audio-free and microphone-free routes. |
| Finish and return | Explicitly save completion, offer the next study day, and allow saving useful words to the personal notebook. Interleaved practice and transfer days reuse words and tasks from earlier lessons. |

The opening preview estimates 12–15 minutes with the added situational practice. Measure actual learner effort on phone and desktop before calibrating the range. [Pacing review](../curriculum/french-lesson-pacing.md) Use short input and simple exchanges at A1, short multi-turn exchanges and practical messages at A2, and connected responses with an unfamiliar detail or a reason at B1. Give learners a fictional name, address or equivalent where a task needs personal details. Use fictional service documents and health or administration scenarios as language activities. The course's spoken activity can be done aloud, silently or on paper. Use existing optional device playback where available. For any required listening task, prepare reviewed audio in advance from the original script, record voice provenance and rights, and bundle the audio locally for web and offline study. Include a transcript and an accessible reading alternative, with the support used recorded if an activity distinguishes it.

### First implementation brief · D003 Spell your name

- **Outcome.** At a fictional neighbourhood workshop reception, respond to a request to spell a name and confirm or correct the name read back.
- **Prerequisites and carryover.** Recycle the polite greeting and name exchange from D001–D002. Teach letters only as needed for the chosen names and model the sounds in short groups. Compare the spelling and clarification entries against the existing foundations and personal-details banks before assigning IDs.
- **Study and practice.** Write an original receptionist exchange using a fictional name, such as Nora. Show the English meaning and a clear written spelling. Practise recognizing the request and selecting the correct spelling. Then let the learner say or type a spelling for another supplied name and use a read-back checklist. Where playback is enabled, verify that each letter is read separately in French.
- **Visual brief.** Generate a neutral French community-centre reception with one adult visitor and one staff member. Keep the scene free of signage/brands and keep names and letters in accessible UI text. If generated art is pending, use a text scene. The picture supplies context, while the exercise answer comes from the spoken or written language.
- **Acceptance.** Access the third day from the existing French course, save an answer, reload to the same day and section, finish the lesson and resume D001 or D002 with their saved state intact. Verify this after a course update and through the documented backup/offline paths. Verify that inherited stock imagery does not appear in the generated-only course surface.

## Build and review in batches

### First five lessons for manual review

Owner scope, 2026-11-22. Stop the opening preview at D005. The existing D001 and D002 count toward the five. Add original D003 **Spell your name**, D004 **Ask someone to repeat**, and D005 **Confirm a time**. Group all five under **Unit 1: Your first conversations in France** within **French A1: First steps in France**. D006 and later days come after owner review.

- **Content.** D003–D005 use original fictional exchanges, direct English translations and canonical vocabulary, followed by three choices and a manual practice prompt. Recycle introductions in the spelling lesson, spelling and names in the repetition lesson, and clarification in the time lesson. Existing Day 1 text credits remain visible.
- **Learner.** Open French → Today → Open daily course → Choose a study day. The unit chooser offers Days 1–5. Daily study keeps the exact day and section across visits and offers the next unfinished day after completion. French daily vocabulary uses text with optional existing word playback. The general library retains its media bindings.
- **Review.** Try spelling and read-back, a slower repetition request, and confirming the time out loud or in the optional writing field. Check the French and English displayed in study and the usefulness of the wrong-answer feedback. Save a word, leave, return to the saved section, and revisit each pilot day. For the local preview and offline download, use the [local operations guide](../LOCAL_OPERATIONS.md#test-the-offline-ready-preview).

### Shorter lesson flow after owner review

The five French drafts use three screens: **Dialogue**, **Quick practice** and **Try it**. Start with the full French transcript and its full English translation. Put one short teaching tip on the dialogue and place canonical words and notebook controls in an optional disclosure. Keep source acknowledgement in the lesson's credit panel. Group the original three practice questions on one screen, use shorter instructions and feedback, and keep a single speaking or writing prompt with an optional model on the final screen.

**Next section** advances and records dialogue study in the same action. Practice responses are optional and retain their own feedback and saved results. **Finish lesson** records study after the dialogue is studied. Free navigation remains available. Consolidate older study, word and dialogue locations into the new dialogue step, and map practice attempts to the grouped practice step. Existing block IDs, answer keys, event scopes and finished days remain usable.

Acceptance for this revision covers all five French dialogues, responsive translations, direct progression, stored practice and resume, original credits, prior progress and the offline preview. The Japanese daily course continues to use its existing activity renderer.

French dialogue design after owner review. The library lesson and daily-course views share a reader that gives both languages a complete transcript. Put French on the left and English on the right when there is space. Stack the complete French transcript before the complete translation on narrow screens. A locally bundled model-generated photo can set the scene. Keep the photo separate from the lesson text and preserve speaker labels, French playback and all practice actions.
