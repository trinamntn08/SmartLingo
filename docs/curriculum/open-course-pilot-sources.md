## Language and pedagogical review

Outcome: greet an adult neighbour in your building, ask how they are, answer briefly and leave politely. Learner role: newcomer. Study shows French and English directly; practice has optional hints and supportive feedback. A typed or spoken personal attempt is self-reviewed and does not receive an invented correctness/pronunciation score.

The source calls its conversational check-in informal. The pilot preserves that distinction and explicitly teaches the existing polite *vous* alternative. Existing canonical vocabulary examples may contain supporting words beyond this day's target phrases; they remain visible with English and are not additional recall requirements. No standalone noun is added; the day's wish explains *la journée* (feminine). *Bonjour* works during the day, including afternoon; *bonsoir* is an evening arrival greeting, with no fixed universal switch time. *Bonne journée* is a departing wish. The familiar greeting is shown for recognition, while the neighbour scenario defaults to polite forms. A familiar greeting is not inherently offensive; feedback must describe suitability rather than label it ungrammatical.

Owner/native-teacher review remains needed for idiomatic dialogue, register choices in the specific neighbour setting, translation nuance, estimated workload and accepted situational alternatives. This narrow reuse of existing foundation vocabulary is not a new broad topic/level research exercise: no claim of triangulation against two additional FLE references or complete CEFR descriptor coverage is made.
