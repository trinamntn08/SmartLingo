# French daily course · B1

Draft plan for English-speaking adults living in France. Days `D113`–`D168` cover eight units. Each unit has five development lessons, a mixed practice lesson, and a new situation to apply what the learner has practised. B1 is an editorial estimate for the tasks in this plan. The [Council of Europe global scale](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale) informs the emphasis on understanding main points, handling travel problems, relating experiences in connected language, and giving brief reasons for a choice.

The learner brings A2 experience with routine exchanges, short messages, simple reasons, comparisons, and common past and future forms. Begin each unit with an example from a familiar situation and recycle its language in a slightly different role or context. Author clear original speech, messages, and dialogues with English support during study. Transfer days use a fresh scenario, a short response the learner can say or write, and a model with several acceptable responses. Record activity completion and keep feedback about the task, such as whether a reason and a next step were clear.

## Unit 1 · Get there when plans change

Compare viable routes, obtain missing information, and tell another person how a disruption changes your arrival. Reuse A2 transport, timetables, and polite clarification. Consider a model-generated station waiting area with fictional travellers and blank signs. Put all route and time information in authored text or audio.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D113 | Understand a travel disruption | Identify what has changed, which part of a route is affected, and when a service is expected to return from a short recorded announcement. | `une interruption`, `en raison de`, `jusqu’à`. Reuse route directions, clock times, and `être annulé`. |
| D114 | Find out what still runs | Ask a staff member whether another service stops at your destination and check where to change. | `est-ce que…`, `si j’ai bien compris`, `une correspondance`. Reuse destination and platform questions. |
| D115 | Compare two ways there | Compare two routes and recommend one for a traveller with a deadline. Give a reason based on changes, total time, or flexibility. | `plus rapide`, `moins direct`, `même si`, `avoir le temps de`. Reuse A2 comparisons and journey duration. |
| D116 | Notify someone of a delay | Send a short message giving the cause of a delay, a revised arrival estimate, and a next step if the journey changes again. | `du coup`, `je devrais arriver`, `si le bus est complet, je prendrai…`. Reuse apology and time phrases. |
| D117 | Respond when the alternative fails | Ask a follow-up when a proposed option is unavailable, then agree on a viable departure with the staff member. | `dans ce cas`, `il reste…`, `le prochain départ`. Reuse `vous pourriez…` and D114 confirmation. |
| D118 | Practise the change of route | Read a timetable, listen for the updated platform, choose an option, and correct a friend's obsolete arrival information. | Interleave time and platform details, reasons, confirmation, and revised plans from D113–D117. |
| D119 | Reach a time-sensitive event | Handle a fictional cancelled train. Choose an alternative for a stated constraint and give a short update that covers the revised arrival time and why you chose that route. | Transfer and review. Check feasibility, a reason tied to the constraint, and an actionable update. |

## Unit 2 · Tell a useful story about your experience

Tell a coherent personal story and respond to a listener's questions. Reuse A2 past events, everyday reactions, and time expressions. Consider a model-generated fictional community kitchen as a setting. Supply all event details in the dialogue or text.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D120 | Set the scene | Introduce where, when, and with whom a small event happened and describe the situation at the start. | `au début`, `ce jour-là`, background with `l’imparfait`. Reuse place descriptions and past time markers. |
| D121 | Explain what happened next | Put three events in order and retell the sequence in a few connected sentences. | `puis`, `ensuite`, `finalement`, `le passé composé` for events. Reuse the background established in D120. |
| D122 | Describe an unexpected moment | Explain what you were doing when something changed and describe how you reacted. | `pendant que`, `tout à coup`, `se rendre compte que`. Contrast background and event through an authored example. |
| D123 | Say what you learned | Give a reaction and explain how the experience changed what you would do next time. | `ce qui m’a surpris`, `depuis`, `la prochaine fois`. Reuse opinion, reasons, and future forms. |
| D124 | Answer a listener's follow-up | Clarify who helped and what happened next when a listener interrupts a story to ask for missing details. | `de qui s’agit-il ?`, `c’était… qui…`, `en fait`. Reuse role vocabulary and the clarification habits from Unit 1. |
| D125 | Practise telling it clearly | Reconstruct an anecdote from a voice note and reorder a draft so a listener can distinguish background, action, and outcome. | Interleave both past forms, time connectors, reactions, and listener questions from D120–D124. |
| D126 | Tell a newcomer about your first volunteer shift | Give a short spoken or written account using original fictional prompts or a personal example, then answer one follow-up question. | Transfer and review. Check the setting, sequence, outcome, and one reason or reflection. |

## Unit 3 · Follow up on a home or service problem

Report the history of a routine issue, negotiate a practical next step, and leave a clear written summary. Reuse A2 service requests, repair vocabulary, and scheduling. Consider a model-generated building hallway with a generic light fixture. Label locations and display all appointment information in accessible text.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D127 | Describe the issue precisely | Tell a building contact what is happening, where the issue is, and whether it happens every time. | `une panne`, `par moments`, `ça se produit quand…`. Reuse rooms, simple descriptions, and frequency. |
| D128 | Give the history so far | State when the problem started, what you already tried, and whether the situation changed. | `depuis`, `il y a`, `j’ai déjà…`, `ça continue`. Reuse the background and event distinction from Unit 2. |
| D129 | Arrange a workable visit | Explain when you can be there, respond to two suggested slots, and propose a workable time. | `un créneau`, `ça m’arrangerait`, `je peux me libérer`. Reuse polite questions and the route comparison pattern from Unit 1. |
| D130 | Ask for a progress update | Refer to an earlier conversation, ask what has been scheduled, and clarify who will contact whom next. | `je me permets de revenir vers vous`, `au sujet de`, `est-ce prévu pour…`. Reuse A2 short emails and times. |
| D131 | Confirm the agreement in writing | Draft a brief message stating the issue, agreed visit time, and one point that still needs confirmation. | `comme convenu`, `je vous confirme que`, `pourriez-vous préciser…`. Reuse D127–D130 issue and scheduling language. |
| D132 | Practise a repair conversation | Compare a voicemail with an email, notice their different dates, and ask for confirmation in an appropriate register. | Interleave past history, ongoing situations, appointment possibilities, and clarification from D127–D131. |
| D133 | Follow up after a missed visit | Respond to a fictional missed repair visit. Give a short timeline and agree on or request a concrete next action. Close with a clear message. | Transfer and review. Check issue history, appropriate tone, and a next action with an owner or time. |

## Unit 4 · Explain work and learning needs

Describe experience, clarify a task, and make a practical proposal in a workplace or adult-learning setting. Reuse A2 course and work exchanges plus B1 experience and scheduling. Consider a model-generated workshop or meeting table with fictional participants and unbranded equipment. Provide all instructions as authored text and speech.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D134 | Describe a skill with an example | Explain a skill relevant to a task and give one specific example of using it. | `être à l’aise avec`, `avoir l’habitude de`, `par exemple`. Reuse course and employment vocabulary and Unit 2 stories. |
| D135 | Check a multi-step instruction | Restate the order of three steps, ask about one missing detail, and confirm a deadline. | `d’abord`, `une fois que`, `il faut rendre…`, `c’est bien ça ?`. Reuse time and clarification. |
| D136 | Explain what is holding you up | State what has been done, what is still difficult, and what support would help. | `j’ai déjà`, `il me reste à`, `j’aurais besoin de`. Reuse Unit 3 status updates and correspondence. |
| D137 | Suggest a practical change | Propose a change to a course or shift arrangement and briefly explain the benefit and a trade-off. | `on pourrait`, `ça permettrait de`, `l’inconvénient, c’est que`. Reuse comparison and reasons from Unit 1. |
| D138 | Confirm the division of tasks | Summarise which person will do each part of a job and by when. Ask for agreement on one point. | `s’occuper de`, `de mon côté`, `d’ici vendredi`, `ça vous convient ?`. Reuse D135 sequencing and D137 proposals. |
| D139 | Practise a brief handover | Read a task note and listen to a colleague's update. Say what was done, what remains, and who will check it. | Interleave task order, responsibility, deadline, and polite requests from D134–D138. |
| D140 | Agree how to finish a group task | Resolve a fictional training-project delay. Describe progress, offer a practical adjustment, and confirm the agreed responsibilities. | Transfer and review. Check clarity about progress, a reasoned proposal, and ownership of the next action. |

## Unit 5 · Understand and relay local information

Identify the main points of a short local notice or report and relay the relevant changes to another person. Reuse A2 announcements, comparisons, and short messages. Consider a model-generated view of a fictional library entrance and a noticeboard without legible print. Display all notices as selectable, author-written text.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D141 | Extract the main point | Choose a one-sentence summary of an original local audio bulletin and identify two useful details. | `désormais`, `à partir de`, `être concerné`. Reuse Unit 1 announcement language and time phrases. |
| D142 | Compare two updates | Read two fictional announcements and identify which details agree and which changed between versions. | `la première annonce`, `la mise à jour`, `auparavant`, `en revanche`. Reuse present and past time reference. |
| D143 | Attribute a statement | Summarise what a notice states and what a resident thinks, keeping the source of each statement clear. | `d’après l’annonce`, `selon`, `elle pense que`, `il précise que`. Reuse reported information and simple opinions. |
| D144 | Explain what it means for a friend | Select details from a closure notice and explain the changed access or hours to someone with a stated need. | `ça veut dire que`, `il faudra`, `si tu viens…`. Reuse conditions and audience-specific advice from Unit 1. |
| D145 | Ask about something still unclear | Identify a detail the notice leaves open and ask a relevant question in a short message. | `pourriez-vous me dire si…`, `je voudrais vérifier`, `je n’ai pas trouvé…`. Reuse Unit 3 follow-up emails. |
| D146 | Practise giving a concise update | Compare an original spoken announcement and a written update, then tell a newcomer what they need to know. | Interleave timing, attribution, relevant detail, and unresolved information from D141–D145. |
| D147 | Pass on a change to a local service | Use two fictional sources to send an update about revised library access, explain how it affects the recipient, and flag a detail still to verify. | Transfer and review. Check the main change, one useful consequence, and clear treatment of uncertainty. |

## Unit 6 · Take part in a neighbourhood discussion

Express an opinion with reasons, respond respectfully to a different view, and help reach a practical decision. Reuse A2 social plans, the proposal patterns from Unit 4, and attribution from Unit 5. Consider a model-generated community room with fictional residents. Present any agenda or proposals as independent, authored text.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D148 | Follow two viewpoints | Listen to two residents discuss possible uses for a shared room and identify their preferences and reasons. | `être pour`, `être favorable à`, `l’idée serait de`. Reuse Unit 5 attribution and opinion language. |
| D149 | Give a reasoned opinion | State a preference for a proposed activity and support it with a concrete reason and example. | `à mon avis`, `ce qui compte pour moi`, `parce que`, `par exemple`. Reuse comparisons and personal priorities. |
| D150 | Respond to a different view | Acknowledge one point and explain a concern before making a suggestion. | `je comprends votre point de vue`, `le souci, c’est que`, `et si on… ?`. Reuse Unit 4 practical proposals. |
| D151 | Compare options under constraints | Compare two proposals for a fictional workshop that have different costs, opening hours, and practical access arrangements. | `ça dépend de`, `plus pratique pour`, `à condition de`. Reuse Unit 1 trade-offs and simple conditions. |
| D152 | Put a compromise into words | State what both people agreed on and propose a change that addresses a remaining concern. | `on est d’accord sur`, `on pourrait essayer`, `pour commencer`. Reuse D150 response strategies and D151 criteria. |
| D153 | Practise a short discussion | Respond to two turns in a fictional discussion and summarise the strongest reason for each option. | Interleave reasons, qualifying a view, polite disagreement, and compromise from D148–D152. |
| D154 | Agree on a community workshop slot | Choose or negotiate a slot after reading two preferences. Give a reason, acknowledge an objection, and confirm a compromise. | Transfer and review. Check reasons, response to the other person, and a specific agreement. |

## Unit 7 · Clarify an appointment and its next steps

Prepare questions, handle a change, and confirm the next action after an appointment. Reuse A2 appointment language and B1 clarification, situation history, and follow-up. Use fictional administrative and health-related scenarios for language practice. A model-generated unbranded waiting room could set the scene. Provide times, instructions, and all documents as original text with fictional details.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D155 | Read a follow-up invitation | Extract the purpose, time, place, and stated preparation from a fictional appointment message. | `un rendez-vous de suivi`, `se présenter`, `apporter`. Reuse A2 appointments and Unit 5 main-point extraction. |
| D156 | Give a short account to the staff member | State the reason for a visit, describe what changed since the first appointment, and ask one question about the plan. | `depuis notre dernier rendez-vous`, `j’ai remarqué que`, `je voudrais savoir…`. Reuse Unit 2 timeline and Unit 3 status language. |
| D157 | Check which step comes first | Rephrase a two-step instruction and ask the staff member to confirm the order or deadline. | `si je résume`, `avant de`, `dans quel délai ?`. Reuse Unit 4 multi-step instructions. |
| D158 | Explain a scheduling conflict | Explain why a new appointment time is difficult and propose two alternatives. | `un empêchement`, `je ne pourrai pas`, `si c’est possible`, `je suis disponible`. Reuse Unit 3 scheduling and Unit 6 compromise. |
| D159 | Write down the agreed next step | Draft a short confirmation that includes the agreed date, what the learner will do, and one open question. | `suite à notre échange`, `je vous confirme`, `il reste un point à préciser`. Reuse Unit 3 follow-up. |
| D160 | Practise finding conflicting details | Compare a fictional confirmation and a voicemail. Spot a changed time or unclear location and ask the appropriate person to clarify. | Interleave invitations, sequences, conflict, and confirmation from D155–D159. |
| D161 | Resolve a changed appointment | Handle a fictional change to a local service appointment. Explain the conflict, negotiate a time, and produce a final note with the next action. | Transfer and review. Check date and time accuracy, a clear clarification, and a follow-up the learner can act on. |

## Unit 8 · Make a complicated week work

Bring together planning, interaction, and follow-up in an original multi-step story about attending an adult-learning workshop. Provide a short recap of returning characters and key details each day. Use a reusable model-generated scene of a fictional training centre, with accessible text for locations and instructions.

| Day | Lesson | Learner task | Language focus and reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D162 | Plan around two commitments | Read a fictional training invitation and appointment plan, identify a conflict, and propose a schedule that keeps both commitments. | Reuse Unit 7 deadlines and Unit 1 route feasibility. Practise `en tenant compte de` as a useful phrase. |
| D163 | Explain your plan to a partner | Give the main information, highlight a constraint, and check which option the partner prefers. | Reuse Unit 5 selective summaries and Unit 6 opinion questions. Practise `ce qui nous arrange`. |
| D164 | React to a venue change | Understand an original venue-change message, verify the new directions, and update the plan. | Reuse Unit 1 disruption and Unit 5 attribution. Practise linking old and new plans with `puisque`. |
| D165 | Agree on the first day’s responsibilities | Give a relevant example of your experience, ask a trainer about the instructions, and confirm who will handle one task. | Reuse Unit 2 experience stories and Unit 4 handover. Practise `si vous êtes d’accord`. |
| D166 | Follow up on a small setback | Describe what happened when a fictional room or material was unavailable, propose a practical action, and confirm it in a short note. | Reuse Unit 3 issue history and Unit 6 compromise. Practise `faute de` in an optional example. |
| D167 | Rehearse a changed plan | Use a new fictional notice and two messages to rebuild a schedule, explain it, and revise a reply after a follow-up. | Interleave details, narrative, options, negotiation, and summaries from the full B1 plan. |
| D168 | Help a newcomer get to their first workshop | Handle a new version of the story. Identify two changes from a written notice and original audio, explain a viable plan, then reply to a question or setback. | Transfer and review. Check factual accuracy, connected explanation, a reason tied to the learner's priorities, and a clear next step. |
